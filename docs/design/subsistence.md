# Making a living: food, work and sickness

Agreed with the owner on 2026-10-26 (questions Q1–Q4), after the live town had
been hungry for days. This comes before Mr Lim's thinking head and marriage.
None of this text goes into a prompt.

## What the living town showed (game day 20757)

- **Food:** about 21 fish a day were caught, 46 of 63 by Rahim, against roughly
  90 a day needed if everyone ate fish. Nobody has ever farmed. There were 33
  meals in a day for 13 people.
- **Money:** 7 of 13 residents had 0¢. There had been one hire ever. Mdm Tan held
  $227 and Ah Huat $90, unspent. Nobody bought at a shop for a whole game day,
  because the shops were empty or priced out (rice at $4.20, against 20¢ off the
  lorry).
- **Food in the wrong hands:** Ah Huat carried 16 fish and cooked none. Mdm Tan
  and Fatimah had rice and water but no fuel to cook it.
- **No cost to starving:** hunger sat at the top for days and nothing changed.

## Q1. The land feeds the poor, a little (yes: small but free)

- **Forage:** a 30-minute chore that gives one thing:
  - at the farm plot's ditches and trees: wild kangkong (vegetables) or a hand of bananas;
  - at the jetty's palms: a coconut.
- At most two forages a day each: a meal or two, not a living. Shops, fishing
  and farming still pay better.
- **New items:** a coconut and a hand of bananas, both eaten as they are, as snacks.

## Q2. Three meals a day

Hunger rises about 1,100 over a day, so each proper meal takes off about a third
of that:

| Kind | Takes off | Food |
|---|---|---|
| A proper meal (5 to 6 hours) | about 330–380 | bubur, fish porridge, vegetable rice, grilled fish, sambal kangkong |
| A snack (2 to 3 hours) | about 100–220 | raw fish, a banana, a coconut, bread, boiled kangkong |

Kopi stays a drink.

## Q3. Money reaches the poor (all three)

1. **Mr Lim's paid odd jobs, scripted, now.**
   - Each morning he pins two or three odd jobs on the board, for example carry
     water to the village head's house, clear the path, or mend the jetty planks.
     Each is done at its place (an hour's chore) and paid on completion from his
     own purse, so the money enters the town through work.
   - Pay is about 50¢ a job, at most one job a day each, so about $1.50 a day comes
     into town.
   - Mr Lim himself is seen pinning them, which starts his visible body.
2. **Pak Osman comes every day, Sundays too** (owner, 2026-10-26), from about half past six to about noon. **Selling to the lorry is made visible.** The horn says what Pak Osman is
   buying as well as what he brings ("…and he's buying fish and vegetables").
3. **Shopkeepers need help.** An owner sees, in money, what the travellers who
   left unserved would have paid ("Travellers who left unserved today would have
   paid about $1.20"), so being away from the counter has a visible cost and a
   helper is worth hiring.

## Q4. Sickness from hunger (yes; no death)

- **Falling ill:** after about a day at the worst of hunger (faint), a resident
  falls ill. They're too weak to work a shift, do chores or fish, they tire quickly,
  and they mostly rest at home.
- **Others see it:** "Aminah looks ill." Kin remember it, as with fainting.
- **Water (amended by the owner, 2026-10-28: "Both"):** ill residents in the
  living town couldn't fetch water, so they couldn't cook and stayed hungry
  and ill (Siti and Mak Esah were told they were short of water ten times and
  more). Two changes:
  - (a) Someone ill may still fetch water, one pail at a time and slowly (half
    an hour instead of ten minutes). Shifts, fishing, foraging, firewood and
    odd jobs stay blocked.
  - (b) Kin and neighbours who can see them, when they have no water with
    them, notice it in-world ("Aminah looked ill, too weak to be fetching
    water."), once a day each, so they can bring some.
- **Getting well:** about six hours after hunger falls below hungry, they're well again.
- **Never death.**
- Being ill is saved state, so it takes the next save version.

## Where cooking happens (owner, 2026-10-26: "all three")

Residents kept inventing cooking that didn't exist ("Hassan's fire" at the
jetty, porridge "already cooked") and paid each other for it. The owner chose all
three answers, so the stories come true when someone does the work:

1. **Outdoor fires are real.**
   - Anyone with a bundle of firewood can light a cooking fire outdoors, at the
     jetty or the beach, and it burns about an hour.
   - Anyone at it can cook simple food from what they carry: grilled fish, or
     bubur if they have a pot.
   - *Drafted:* a cooking pot is a new item, sold off the lorry for a few cents, and lasts.
2. **Stoves and the kopitiam stay where cooking otherwise happens,** and the notes
   make it plain: a resident sees a fire or a stove only where one is really burning.
3. **A shared village kitchen.** Mr Lim builds it through paid odd jobs, as with
   the school. Anyone can cook there, bringing their own ingredients and a few
   cents of firewood or fuel.
